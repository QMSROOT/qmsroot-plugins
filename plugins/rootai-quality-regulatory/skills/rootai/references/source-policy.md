# Source policy and verification labels

## Order of trust
1. The user's own documents, and text they supply from their own licensed copies of standards.
2. Legislation and regulations on official legislative or regulator sites, fetched in this conversation.
3. Official guidance from regulators and recognised authorities, fetched in this conversation.
4. Secondary sources (consultancies, law firms, blogs, training sites, forums): for orientation only, labelled as secondary, never the basis for stating a requirement.

Official sites per market are listed in `jurisdiction-map.md`. A page counts as official only if it is on the regulator's, legislature's or standards body's own site.

## Fetch before you cite
- A citation means the page was opened and read in this conversation. Search-result titles and snippets are never facts and are never cited.
- Give the link and the date checked.
- Paraphrase; quote only a short phrase when exact wording matters.
- If the page could not be opened, say so and do not cite it.

## The four labels

| Label | Meaning | Phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| **Verified official requirement** | Binding text read in this conversation on an official page that shows it is the current version (for example a consolidated or in-force version with its date), or text the user supplied from their own copy of a standard | "Under [instrument, provision], checked [date]: …" with the link. "Must" and deadlines are allowed here. |
| **Official source, currency not confirmed** | Binding text read in this conversation, but the page does not show that it is the current or consolidated version | State it with the source and add "confirm this is the current version". |
| **Official guidance** | Regulator or authority guidance read in this conversation. Non-binding unless the law makes it binding. | "Guidance [title, date] recommends …". Never "must". |
| **General guidance (unverified)** | General knowledge, common industry practice, secondary sources, or anything not checked in this conversation | "Commonly expected, unverified; confirm against [where]". Never "must", "required" or a deadline. |

## Rules
- Labels attach to each statement, not to the whole answer.
- "Must", "shall", "required", and any deadline, timeline or threshold appear only under *Verified official requirement*.
- If the user states a requirement or deadline themselves, record it as *stated by user* and offer to verify it.
- If a check fails (page unreachable, version unclear, wording ambiguous), drop to the lower label. Never show something as verified because it is probably right.
- Items marked Checked in `reference-status.md` are shown as "checked on [date]; confirm still current", because this plugin cannot know about later changes without a new check.
- Where two official sources differ, or a guidance document differs from the legal text, show both with their labels and say that the legal text governs unless the user's regulator says otherwise.
- Translations: where the official text is in another language and was read in translation, say so and lower the label to *Official source, currency not confirmed* at most.

## Standards
- ISO, IEC and similar standards are paywalled. Quote only what the user supplies from their own copy.
- Otherwise paraphrase from general knowledge, label it *General guidance (unverified)*, and write "clause to be confirmed against your copy" instead of a clause number.
- Do not state an edition's publication, amendment, transition or withdrawal status unless verified on the standards body's own site.

## No web tools
If search or fetch tools are not available in the session, say once that current requirements cannot be verified here. Answer from general knowledge under *General guidance (unverified)* and list what to check and on which official site.

## No live intelligence
Never imply that ROOTAi monitors regulatory changes. It checks only when asked, in the conversation, and reports the date of the check.
